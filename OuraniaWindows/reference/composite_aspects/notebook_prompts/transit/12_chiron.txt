===== chiron 1 of 29 =====
Using the source titled Ourania writing brief, write the 15 readings listed below. Each describes a period of time: a moving body in the sky acting on a point in the relationship's chart.
For each reading give exactly three parts, in this order, as plain text:
1. the heading, copied exactly as I list it, on a line by itself
2. one lead sentence on its own line that states the whole dynamic plainly
3. one paragraph of three to five sentences
Rules: 65 to 90 words per reading. No bullet points, no bold, no quotation marks, no dashes, no citations. Readings that share the same two bodies must use completely different sentences from one another: do not repeat a definition or a closing line. Use the meanings of the bodies and of each aspect given in the brief. Write nothing before the first heading or after the last paragraph.
Avoid these habits, which make the readings sound machine-made: do not begin a paragraph by naming the aspect (A square between, Under a trine between, An opposition between); do not end on advice that starts with an -ing word (Continuing to, Practicing, Learning to, Maintaining); vary how each reading opens and closes. A sextile is NOT a mild trine: it is a chance that only pays off when the pair makes an effort, and lies idle otherwise, so say what the effort is. Use plain words about ordinary life (meals, money, rows, relatives, work, bed) and do not use: sacred, holy, spiritual, sanctuary, catalyst, evolutionary, profound, dynamic, vibrant, effortless, harmony, journey, destiny. Never write a sentence whose job is to define a body (Eris embodies X, while Mercury governs Y). Do not start a paragraph with the name of the first body followed by a verb like embodies, represents, channels, drives or governs. Start inside the couple's life instead: what the two of them do, argue about or avoid. Let what each body means show through that, in different words every time. Do not start a second sentence with the aspect name (A square, An opposition), and do not give every trine the same shape (Because this comes easily, you risk).
Name both bodies in every reading, at least once each, in passing and never as a definition: for example, Eris's bluntness lands on Mercury's everyday talk. A reader must be able to tell from the paragraph alone which two bodies it is about.
Each aspect must not have its own fixed mould. In the last attempt nearly every sextile lead ended when you actively, every trine had Because this flows so smoothly, you might, most conjunctions turned on However, most squares had Arguments erupt and The pair struggles to, and most oppositions had One partner may, while the other and You risk. Use each of those phrases at most once per block, and build each reading's sentences differently from the one before it. Do not open more than one paragraph per block with The pair or The relationship. If a word is on the avoid list, rewrite the sentence in plain words; do not swap in an invented word (momentums is not a word). Also avoid effortlessly and catalytic.
