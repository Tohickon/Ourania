=ceres conjunction eris
This relationship feeds the people nobody else invited.
Eris stands with the excluded and says so loudly. Ceres provides. Joined, they make the two of you protective of outsiders and quick to notice who has been left without a share. Care has a political edge here. Meals and households become places where fairness is argued out. The combination can also turn nurturing into a quarrel, with help given pointedly, as a rebuke to someone else.

=ceres sextile eris
Standing up for someone is one of the ways this relationship can care.
Ceres looks after and Eris speaks out. The sextile lets them cooperate when a situation calls for it. If one of you, or someone close, is being overlooked or treated unfairly, the pair can provide support and say what needs saying. It is an occasional tool more than a daily habit, and all the more effective for being used sparingly.

=ceres square eris
Care and conflict keep getting mixed up.
Ceres wants everyone fed and settled. Eris wants the unfairness named first. Squared, family meals, shared resources and questions of who looks after whom become occasions for dispute. One of you may feel left out of the care, and say so sharply. The grievance is usually genuine and badly timed. Hearing it outside the kitchen, so to speak, lets both the care and the complaint be dealt with properly.

=ceres trine eris
This relationship looks after the left-out as a matter of course.
Nurture and plain speaking go together without strain. The two of you make room for the awkward guest and the difficult relative, and can raise a matter of fairness without spoiling the meal. Others feel that honesty is safe in your house. The one thing to check is that the pair's sympathy for outsiders does not leave its own members last in the queue.

=ceres opposition eris
One of you keeps the peace at home and the other disturbs it.
Ceres holds the household together and Eris asks who was not consulted. Opposed, one partner becomes the provider of comfort and the other the voice of complaint. The provider finds the other ungrateful, and the complainer finds the comfort a way of smoothing over wrongs. There is truth on both sides. Comfort that can hear a complaint is worth more, and a complaint made with some care is more likely to be heard.

=ceres conjunction eros
Desire and tenderness are closely joined in this relationship.
Eros is passionate attraction and Ceres is care for the body. Together they make physical love nurturing and nurture sensual. The two of you may express desire through feeding, bathing and tending each other, and feel most wanted when most looked after. It is a warm and earthy bond. The line between lover and parent can blur, which sometimes cools the very passion it began with.

=ceres sextile eros
Looking after each other's bodies can kindle attraction here.
Ceres tends and Eros desires. The sextile links them by invitation. Care given with attention, a meal prepared, a tired back rubbed, readily turns toward intimacy when both wish it. It is a simple way of keeping warmth alive. It has to be offered as care first, and allowed to become more only if it does.

=ceres square eros
Being mothered and being wanted get in each other's way.
Eros wants a lover and Ceres offers a caretaker. Squared, the two of you can fall into roles that kill desire: one tending, the other tended. Or passion may be pursued at the expense of ordinary kindness. Food and sex can both become ways of managing each other. It helps to notice which is being asked for at a given moment, comfort or desire, and answer that one.

=ceres trine eros
Passion and care feed each other in this relationship.
The two of you move easily between looking after one another and wanting one another, and neither spoils the other. Ceres makes desire feel safe and Eros keeps care from becoming routine. Physical life is affectionate and sustaining. The comfort is real. It can make the pair a little self-enclosed, content with each other's company to the point of needing no one else.

=ceres opposition eros
One of you offers care and the other offers desire.
Ceres and Eros face each other, and their gifts get divided. One partner shows love by providing and the other by wanting. Each may feel the other is missing the point. The provider longs to be desired and the lover longs to be looked after, though neither says it. Asking for what you usually give is the quickest way to change the pattern.

=ceres conjunction hygiea
Health is how this relationship shows that it cares.
Ceres feeds and Hygiea keeps the body well. Together they focus the pair on diet, rest, cleanliness and prevention. The two of you look after each other's wellbeing attentively, and a well-kept, wholesome household matters to you. It is a sound basis for a long life together. It can become anxious. Concern for health turns into monitoring, and food into a matter of rules.

=ceres sextile hygiea
Good habits are easy for this relationship to adopt once it decides to.
Ceres provides nourishment and Hygiea knows what is wholesome. With a sextile, the two of you can improve how you eat, sleep and recover without much struggle, each encouraging the other. The gain is practical and steady. It depends on a decision, and on someone going first.

=ceres square hygiea
Comfort and discipline disagree about what is good for you.
Ceres says eat, rest, be comforted. Hygiea says moderate, cleanse, take care. Squared, the two of you may quarrel over food and health, with one indulging and the other correcting. Care feels like criticism. Illness or diet can become a point of control. What helps is agreeing on a few shared habits and leaving each other's plates alone otherwise.

=ceres trine hygiea
This relationship keeps itself well without making a project of it.
Nourishment and good sense work together. The two of you tend to eat properly, rest when tired and notice early when something is amiss, and you do it for each other without nagging. The household is healthy in an unforced way. That is worth valuing. It needs only a little vigilance in hard times, when the routine slips first.

=ceres opposition hygiea
One of you comforts and the other corrects.
Ceres offers the second helping and Hygiea counts it. Opposed, one partner becomes the indulgent provider and the other the guardian of health. Each undermines the other and feels undermined. The comforter is called careless and the guardian is called joyless. A household needs both pleasure and prudence, and it is better when each of you supplies some of each.

=ceres conjunction juno
Commitment in this relationship means looking after each other.
Juno is the partnership as a lasting agreement and Ceres is daily provision. Joined, they make care part of the vow. The two of you understand loyalty as showing up: feeding, nursing, sharing the load. It gives the bond great practical solidity. The risk is that the marriage becomes a household and little else, with partners turning into co-parents or co-managers.

=ceres sextile juno
Acts of care can reinforce this relationship's commitment.
Ceres tends and Juno binds. The sextile lets one strengthen the other when the two of you make the effort. Looking after each other in concrete ways, especially when it is inconvenient, makes the promise feel real. It is a quiet resource. Neglected, the commitment stays a matter of words.

=ceres square juno
Who looks after whom is a point of contention in this partnership.
Juno wants an agreement between equals. Ceres gives as a parent gives, without counting. Squared, the two of you may disagree about how much care is owed and whether it is fairly shared. One feels like the household's servant, or like a child being managed. Children or dependants can come between you. The terms of care need stating as plainly as any other part of the bargain.

=ceres trine juno
Loyalty and care strengthen each other here.
The two of you keep faith by looking after one another, and it comes naturally. Ceres gives the commitment warmth and Juno gives the care a frame that lasts. Burdens are shared without much bookkeeping. This is the stuff of durable partnerships. It is only necessary to keep some part of the bond that is not about duty at all.

=ceres opposition juno
One of you is the parent and the other the partner.
Ceres and Juno face each other, and the relationship is pulled between nurturing and equal alliance. One of you may be more absorbed in caring, for children, family or the other's needs, while the other wants a spouse and a peer. Each feels neglected in a different way. The balance returns when time is made for the two of you as a couple, apart from everyone you look after.

=ceres conjunction nessus
Care in this relationship touches old harm.
Nessus marks patterns of damage handed down, often around what should have been safe. Ceres is the parent and provider. Joined, they bring up histories of neglect, smothering or care that came with a price. The two of you may be determined to do better, and that determination is the best of this contact. The old pattern tends to reappear in subtle form, so it needs watching with honesty.

=ceres sextile nessus
This relationship can use care to help end an old pattern.
Nessus shows where harm repeats, and Ceres offers steady, ordinary kindness. The sextile lets the second work on the first. Consistent looking-after, given freely, gradually corrects what earlier experience taught about care. It is slow and it works. The two of you have to keep choosing it, especially on the days when the old reflex is strong.

=ceres square nessus
Nurture and old injury are painfully entangled.
Ceres reaches out to care and Nessus remembers care that hurt. Squared, kindness can be mistrusted, tested or turned into a means of control, and each of you may replay a role from the family you came from. Guilt and obligation do the damage. This is delicate ground, and it may need a third party. The aim is care with no hook in it.

=ceres trine nessus
This relationship can face the damage done in the name of care and still be kind.
Ceres and Nessus cooperate. The two of you are able to talk about family harm calmly and to recognise an unhealthy pattern of dependence when it starts. That awareness protects the tenderness between you. It is a strength. Complacency is the only real risk, the assumption that because the pattern is understood it cannot recur.

=ceres opposition nessus
One of you cares and the other suspects the care.
Ceres offers and Nessus flinches. Opposed, one partner takes the role of giver while the other, for reasons older than the relationship, reads giving as a trap. The giver is hurt and the wary one feels cornered. Sometimes the giving does carry a hidden demand. Honesty from both, about motive on one side and fear on the other, is the way out.

=ceres conjunction pallas
This relationship looks after things intelligently.
Pallas is skill, strategy and clear judgment, and Ceres is care. Together they make nurture practical and well planned. The two of you are good at organising a household, managing someone's treatment, or teaching a child, and you take pride in doing it competently. Care is shown through problem-solving. It can leave feeling out of account, with a solution offered where sympathy was wanted.

=ceres sextile pallas
A good plan can make this relationship's caring more effective.
Ceres wants to help and Pallas knows how to go about it. With a sextile, the two of you can think through a caring task together and do it well. Illness, schooling, a tight budget all yield to this. It requires sitting down and working it out. The feeling and the method are both present and need introducing to each other.

=ceres square pallas
Heart and head disagree about how to help.
Ceres responds with comfort and Pallas with analysis. Squared, one of you tends while the other strategises, and each thinks the other is missing what matters. The carer seems sentimental and the planner seems cold. Disputes about raising children or handling a relative's needs are typical. Both are trying to help. Deciding first what the person actually needs, comfort or a plan, settles most of it.

=ceres trine pallas
Kindness and good sense work together in this relationship.
The two of you care wisely. Ceres supplies the warmth and Pallas the judgment, so help is both meant and well aimed. Practical difficulties are handled with calm competence, and those you look after are in good hands. It is a capable pairing. The only thing to guard is the assumption that you always know best what others need.

=ceres opposition pallas
One of you tends and the other thinks it through.
Ceres acts from the heart and Pallas from the head. Opposed, they settle on different partners. One provides the meals and the comfort while the other provides the plan. Each can feel the other's contribution is the lesser. In fact a household run on one alone is either chaotic or chilly. The division works when both jobs are respected and sometimes exchanged.

=ceres conjunction fortune
This relationship prospers through what it nurtures.
The Part of Fortune marks where ease and material wellbeing come from. Ceres on it ties them to food, land, care and anything that grows by being tended. The two of you do well when you look after things patiently. There is usually enough on the table. The comfort is real and it rewards steadiness more than speed.

=ceres sextile fortune
Tending things carefully tends to bring this relationship good fortune.
Ceres is the pair's capacity to sustain, and the Part of Fortune its natural luck. The sextile means that patient, practical care opens modest doors. A garden, a kitchen, a person looked after well, each has a way of returning something. The link is gentle. It rewards the effort when the effort is made.

=ceres square fortune
Looking after others and enjoying good fortune compete here.
The Part of Fortune offers ease and pleasure. Ceres has duties. Squared, the two of you may find that caring responsibilities eat into comfort, or that comfort is taken with a guilty eye on those who need you. Resources feel stretched between giving and enjoying. A deliberate share set aside for your own pleasure is not selfish in this case. It keeps the giving from turning bitter.

=ceres trine fortune
Care and good luck go together in this relationship.
The two of you provide for each other easily and seem to be provided for in turn. Ceres and the Part of Fortune agree, so that home is comfortable, food is plentiful and there is a general sense of being well supplied. It is a happy placement. Gratitude suits it, along with some thought for where the surplus might do good.

=ceres opposition fortune
One of you does the providing and the other enjoys the luck.
Ceres works to sustain and the Part of Fortune simply receives. Opposed, the two can seem unfairly distributed, with one partner labouring over care while ease falls to the other. Resentment is possible. So is a useful exchange, since the fortunate one can lighten the carer's load and the carer can give the luck some purpose.

=ceres conjunction spirit
This relationship chooses to make care its purpose.
The Part of Spirit is deliberate intention, what a pair sets out to do. Ceres joined to it directs that intention toward nurturing. The two of you may decide to raise a family, grow food, or look after people in some organised way, and bring real will to it. It is a purposeful kind of tenderness. Care that is a mission can become relentless, and needs a day off.

=ceres sextile spirit
Care can be given a clear direction in this relationship.
The Part of Spirit is the pair's conscious aim and Ceres its ability to sustain. With a sextile, the two of you can decide where your looking-after is best spent and follow through. It turns general goodwill into something definite. The step from wishing to deciding is yours to take.

=ceres square spirit
What this relationship intends and whom it must care for are at odds.
The Part of Spirit sets a chosen course. Ceres answers to need as it arises. Squared, plans are interrupted by caring duties, and duties are resented as obstacles. One of you may feel that ambition is being sacrificed, or that it is being put ahead of people. The conflict returns until the plan is redrawn to include those who depend on you.

=ceres trine spirit
Purpose and care support each other here.
The two of you find that what you mean to accomplish and what you naturally look after are much the same. Ceres gives the pair's intentions a human warmth, and the Part of Spirit gives its caring a sense of direction. Work done together has heart. There is little to warn against, beyond taking on more than two people can sustain.

=ceres opposition spirit
One of you follows the plan and the other minds the people.
The Part of Spirit pushes ahead with intention and Ceres stays with those who need tending. Across the relationship, one partner becomes the driver of projects and the other the keeper of the household. Each thinks their own task the essential one. They depend on each other entirely. Acknowledging that, aloud, takes much of the strain out of the division.

=ceres conjunction pholus
In this relationship a small act of care can change a great deal.
Pholus is the small cause with large effects. Ceres is nurture. Together they make the two of you unusually sensitive to what is taken in: food, drink, medicine, kindness. A modest change in how you look after each other can transform the whole atmosphere. It works in both directions, and a small neglect can have large results as well. Old family matters about care may suddenly come to light.

=ceres sextile pholus
A small change in care can open up something larger here.
Ceres tends and Pholus releases. With a sextile, the two of you can use a simple gesture, a shared meal, a question asked kindly, to begin a conversation that has been waiting a long time. The effect is out of proportion to the effort. It is worth trying when something feels stuck.

=ceres square pholus
Matters of care are apt to blow up unexpectedly.
Ceres wants things kept steady and Pholus takes the lid off. Squared, a small dispute about food, health or family can set off a much larger release of feeling than anyone expected. Old grief or resentment about being looked after, or not, comes pouring out. Substances can play a part. Going slowly, and not opening everything at once, keeps the consequences manageable.

=ceres trine pholus
This relationship lets go of old burdens about care with relative ease.
Ceres and Pholus cooperate. The two of you can release long-held family patterns around food, dependence and loss without great upheaval, often through some ordinary event that proves to be a turning point. Change in the household comes naturally. The process is kind. It only asks that you notice when it is happening and allow it.

=ceres opposition pholus
One of you keeps things steady and the other sets things off.
Ceres maintains and Pholus uncorks. Opposed, one partner becomes the guardian of routine and the other the one whose small action brings on a flood. The guardian feels the work undone and the other is astonished at the result. What comes out was already there. Dealing with it together is better than blaming whoever happened to open it.

=ceres conjunction south_node
Nurturing is the pattern this relationship falls into without thinking.
The South Node is what comes automatically, and Ceres there makes it caretaking. The two of you slip into feeding, tending and worrying over each other as if you had always done it. It is comfortable and competent. It can also be a retreat, with the familiar business of looking after used to avoid the less familiar growth that lies at the other end of the axis.

=ceres sextile south_node
The pair has an old fund of caring skill to draw on.
Ceres links easily to the South Node's store of past experience. The two of you know how to look after people, and can call on that when it is needed without having to learn it. Because the sextile also reaches toward the North Node, this skill can serve the relationship's growth. Used that way it is an asset, and no kind of anchor.

=ceres square south_node
Caring duties cut across both the old pattern and the new aim.
Ceres squares the nodal axis from this side as from the other. The work of tending fits neither where the two of you have been nor where you are going, and so it can feel like a constant interruption. Each of you may handle it differently. The care is still owed. Finding a place for it in the plan is part of what the relationship has to work out.

=ceres trine south_node
Looking after each other is second nature here.
Ceres and the South Node agree, so nurture flows from habit. The two of you settle into mutual care quickly and it feels like something remembered. The comfort is real and worth having. Since it costs nothing, it can stand in for effort elsewhere, and the pair may need to push itself toward whatever is less practised.

=ceres opposition south_node
Learning to nurture is the direction this relationship is growing in.
Opposite the South Node, Ceres stands with the North Node. The habits the two of you bring are likely to be independent or detached ones. What is being asked for is patience, tending, and willingness to be needed. It may feel unfamiliar and even burdensome at first. Each time it is chosen, the relationship becomes more of what it is meant to be.

=ceres conjunction vesta
Care in this relationship is given with complete dedication.
Vesta is the tended flame, focused and devoted. Ceres is the provider. Joined, they make looking after the home, the family or a chosen charge into something close to a vocation. The two of you take it seriously and do it thoroughly. Others can rely on you absolutely. The devotion can crowd out lightness, and the carers themselves may go untended.

=ceres sextile vesta
Focus can make this relationship's caring count for more.
Ceres gives care and Vesta gives concentration. The sextile lets the two of you put your whole attention on one thing that needs tending and do it properly. A sick relative, a new child, a demanding season at home all benefit. It is a matter of choosing where to direct the effort, and then keeping to it.

=ceres square vesta
Devotion to a task and care for people compete for the same hours.
Vesta asks for single-minded focus. Ceres asks who needs feeding. Squared, one of you may withdraw into work or practice while the other is left holding the household, or care for others may leave no time for what each of you holds sacred. Both feel neglected in turn. It is eased by protecting a small fixed time for each claim.

=ceres trine vesta
Dedication and nurture are well matched here.
The two of you look after what matters with steady, wholehearted attention. Ceres supplies the warmth and Vesta the constancy. The home has the feel of a place that is kept on purpose. Work done for others is done well and without complaint. It only remains to see that the keepers of the flame are warmed by it too.

=ceres opposition vesta
One of you tends the people and the other tends the flame.
Ceres gives outwardly and Vesta keeps to her own centre. Opposed, one partner becomes the one who looks after everybody, and the other the one who is absorbed in work, study or devotion. The carer feels alone with the load and the devoted one feels constantly interrupted. Each guards something valuable. A fair exchange of time lets both be kept.
